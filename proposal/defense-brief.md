## Explanation for the author and proposal defense

Study one main-agent/subagent analytics hierarchy, with separate evidence/experience,
computation and review children. Correct execution does not prove that the applicable
business meaning was implemented. DataPilot and DS-STAR are conceptual anchors;
FinHelm supplies the adopted infrastructure adaptation path, subject to offline gates.
This is not reproduction of those systems' published scores.

Two mechanisms are author-adopted: check retrieved procedures against current sources
before admitting them to the solver, and challenge candidate interpretations with
source-grounded discriminating fixtures. Both are fallible. Shared scope/version
filters, ordinary review and identical information remain in the baseline.

Four descriptive conditions are Baseline, Evidence-checked experience reuse,
Source-grounded counterexample review and Both. They explicitly supersede the old C1/V2 candidate; old IDs retain historical
meanings. Strong single-agent controls include ordinary and both-mechanism procedures.
Compare final task quality, reuse decisions, common-candidate review errors and costs.
A combined win does not establish positive interaction. MAST describes observable
failures; causal claims require controlled comparisons.

Native DABstep and separately labelled diagnostic/transfer cases remain distinct.
The fee/null walkthrough illustrates discrimination, not natural failure prevalence.
No hidden evaluator rules, reference answers or researcher witnesses enter agent tools
or memory. No agent experiments have run. Full preparation/adaptation/runtime costs
provide the MIB perspective without a promised universal deployment or observed ROI.

## One-minute oral explanation

I study whether two targeted changes improve a hierarchical agent system for business
analytics. First, the system checks whether a remembered procedure applies under the
current documents before using it. Second, the reviewer constructs small test data
that exposes the consequences of a disputed business interpretation. I compare each
change and their combination against ordinary review and similarly equipped single
agents. The aim is to measure reliability together with preparation and operating
costs, and identify when extra checking helps or is unnecessary. These are planned
experiments using public data; no improvement is assumed.
