## Explanation for the author and proposal defense

We study when independent executable verification and explicit alignment of business
context improve the reliability of a fixed multi-agent analytics team, separately and
jointly. Correct execution alone does not establish that the selected business rule
is applicable. For example, a fraud metric can be computed correctly by transaction
count even when the governing document requires an amount-weighted proportion.
This is an illustrative example, not an observed experimental failure.

V2 independently reconstructs and checks a calculation. C1 makes agents record and
reconcile metric definitions, periods, populations, units, exclusions and source
evidence. Shared memory initially means a structured record within one task, not
cross-task learning. Neither mechanism is presumed effective.

The four conditions V0/V2/C1/V2+C1 distinguish the effects of the two interventions.
Paired consistent/conflicting documents with unchanged tables isolate documentary
inconsistency. All systems receive equivalent information and executable tools.
A strong single-agent control tests whether a team is justified; an extra-compute
control addresses improvements caused simply by additional resources. A combined win
alone does not establish interaction. Gold stays outside solver and verifier access.

The business motivation is allocating checking effort between calculations and
business definitions. Measure reliability together with time and cost. Do not claim
realized monetary savings or analyst-time savings without workplace measurement.
The proposed empirical contribution is evidence about effects, costs and boundary
conditions, rather than the invention of agents, verification or document grounding.
The closest-work comparison still needs careful treatment in the proposal.

The benchmark audit recommends a DABstep derivative as the pilot foundation. Final
admission depends on task semantics and reference audits. Controlled adaptations
support interpretable comparisons but limit generalization to natural enterprise
archives. Later transfer testing is planned. Audit calculations demonstrate source
feasibility; no agent experiments or treatment findings exist yet.

## One-minute oral explanation

I study the reliability of multi-agent systems in business analytics, where a correct
answer depends on both tables and rules in documents. A system may execute a calculation
correctly while applying the wrong metric definition. I compare independent executable
verification and structured business-context alignment, separately and together,
under consistent and conflicting documentation. The experiment includes a strong
single-agent baseline and resource controls. The goal is to identify when these
mechanisms help, what they cost, and which errors remain, without assuming that more
agents or more checking necessarily produces better results.
